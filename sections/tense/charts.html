Conditional Perfect — “would have” done

Used for hypothetical actions that would have happened. haber (conditional) + past participle.

Keep haber in the infinitive pattern for the conditional auxiliary (habr- + conditional endings), then add the past participle.

Remove the AR, ER or IR ending from the main verb to form the past participle (-ado / -ido).

Imperative (Commands — Affirmative & Negative)

Used for direct commands. Tú, vosotros, and usted(es) forms differ. (Negative tú uses subjunctive.)

Remove the AR, ER or IR ending from the infinitive, then add the command ending. Formal and all negative commands use present-subjunctive endings.

Affirmative tú (familiar)
Verb type Ending Example
-AR -a ah habla
-ER -e eh come
-IR -e eh vive
Affirmative vosotros (Spain)
Verb type Ending Example
-AR -ad ahd hablad
-ER -ed ehd comed
-IR -id eed vivid
Usted / Ustedes (formal) & Negative commands

Formal commands and all negative commands use the present subjunctive:

Person -AR -ER / -IR
usted -e (hable) -a (coma, viva)
ustedes -en (hablen) -an (coman, vivan)
tú (negative) -es (no hables) -as (no comas, no vivas)

Imperfect Subjunctive (Past uncertainty, “if” clauses)

Used for hypothetical or uncertain past situations, often after “if” or expressions of emotion in the past.

Take the 3rd-person plural preterite, drop -ron, then add the endings. Two forms are accepted; the -ra form is most common.

-AR Endings

tú		 -arais / -aseis AH-reyes / AH-seys
vosotros/as
-ara / -ase AH-rah / AH-seh
él, ella, usted		 -aran / -asen AH-rahn / AH-sehn
ellos, ellas, ustedes
-ER / -IR Endings

Past Perfect Subjunctive (Pluperfect Subjunctive — “had” done, subjunctive)

Used for a completed action before another past point in subjunctive / hypothetical clauses (often with si or emotion in the past). Formed with haber (imperfect subjunctive) + past participle.

Take the 3rd-person plural preterite of haber (hubieron), drop -ron, then add -ra / -se endings. Remove AR, ER or IR from the main verb for the participle (-ado / -ido).

Progressive Tenses (estar + gerund)

Used for ongoing actions (I am doing, I was doing). Conjugate estar + gerund.

Remove the AR, ER or IR ending from the infinitive to form the gerund.

Gerund (present participle)
Verb type Gerund ending Example
-AR -ando AHN-doh hablando
-ER -iendo YEHN-doh comiendo
-IR -iendo YEHN-doh viviendo

Present progressive: estoy hablando — Imperfect: estaba hablando — Future: estaré hablando
