Imperfect

Imperfect Tense

The imperfect describes ongoing, habitual, or background actions in the past (what used to happen, was happening, or how things were).

Quick start
Idea Spanish term Example Pronunciation English
Dictionary form infinitive hablar ah-BLAHR = to speak ah-BLAHR to speak
Who was doing it subject yo yoh, tú too, él ehl… yoh, too, ehl
Ending that shows person + past ongoing conjugation hablaba ah-BLAH-bah = I was speaking / I used to speak ah-BLAH-bah I was speaking / used to speak

Three verb families (by infinitive ending)

Type Ending Example Pronunciation English

-er -er comer koh-MEHR koh-MEHR to eat
-ir -ir vivir bee-BEER bee-BEER to live

Imperfect endings (regular)

Person (Subject Pronoun) -ar -er / -ir Pronunciation pattern
yo yoh (I) -aba -ía -aba = AH-bah; -ía = EE-ah
tú too (you, informal singular) -abas -ías -abas = AH-bahs; -ías = EE-ahs
él ehl / ella EH-yah / usted oos-TEHD -aba -ía same as yo
nosotros noh-SOH-trohs / nosotras noh-SOH-trahs -ábamos -íamos AH-bah-mohs / EE-ah-mohs
vosotros boh-SOH-trohs / vosotras boh-SOH-trahs (Spain) -abais -íais AH-bah-ees / EE-ah-ees
ellos EH-yohs / ellas EH-yahs / ustedes oos-TEH-dehs -aban -ían AH-bahn / EE-ahn

Key points

  • Very regular — only three verbs are irregular: ir eer, ser sehr, ver behr.
  • Used for descriptions, habits, age, time, and ongoing past actions.
