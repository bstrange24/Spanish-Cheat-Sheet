Present Subjunctive

The present subjunctive is used for wishes, doubts, emotions, recommendations, and after certain conjunctions (when the action is not a statement of fact).

Quick start
Idea Spanish term Example Pronunciation English
Dictionary form infinitive hablar ah-BLAHR = to speak ah-BLAHR to speak
Who / what is involved subject yo yoh, tú too, él ehl… yoh, too, ehl
Ending that shows subjunctive conjugation hable AH-bleh = that I speak / I speak (subj.) AH-bleh that I speak

Present Subjunctive endings (regular)

Person (Subject Pronoun) -ar -er / -ir Pronunciation pattern
yo yoh (I) -e -a -e = eh; -a = ah
tú too (you, informal singular) -es -as -es = ehs; -as = ahs
él ehl / ella EH-yah / usted oos-TEHD -e -a same as yo
nosotros noh-SOH-trohs / nosotras noh-SOH-trahs -emos -amos EH-mohs / AH-mohs
vosotros boh-SOH-trohs / vosotras boh-SOH-trahs (Spain) -éis -áis EHS / AHS
ellos EH-yohs / ellas EH-yahs / ustedes oos-TEH-dehs -en -an ehn / ahn

Key points

  • The vowel is the opposite of the present indicative (-ar verbs take -e, -er/-ir take -a).
  • Used after expressions of desire, emotion, doubt, impersonal phrases, and many conjunctions (que, para que, antes de que, etc.).
  • Many common verbs have stem changes or are irregular (tener → tenga, hacer → haga, ir → vaya, ser → sea, etc.).
