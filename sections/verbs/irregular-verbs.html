Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs do not follow the regular -ar / -er / -ir patterns.

Irregular verbs — rules & conjugation

There are four main types of irregularity in the present tense:

Type Pattern Example
1. Stem-changing (boot verbs) The stem vowel changes in all forms except nosotros/vosotros pensar (e→ie), poder (o→ue), pedir (e→i)
2. Irregular "yo" Only the yo form is irregular; all other forms follow regular patterns conocer → conozco, poner → pongo
3. Highly irregular All or most forms change unpredictably ser, estar, ir, haber
4. Prefix-counting Verbs with prefixes that follow the pattern of their base verb contener (from tener), componer (from poner)
Stem-changing verbs (boot verbs) — rules

Rules for Stem-Changing Verbs

  • The last vowel of the stem changes
  • The change happens in all forms except nosotros and vosotros
  • Called "boot verbs" because the changed forms make a boot shape in the conjugation table

Three Types of Stem Changes:

o → ue In -ar, -er, and -ir verbs poder, volver, dormir
e → i Usually in -ir verbs only pedir, seguir, repetir
u → ue Rare — only jugar jugar (the only u→ue verb)

Irregular "yo" verbs — rules

Rules for Irregular "yo" Verbs

  • Only the yo form is irregular
  • All other forms (tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros, vosotros, ellos/ellas/ustedes) follow regular patterns
  • Two main patterns: -zco and -go
  • For verbs that end in -guir, the yo form ends in go
  • For verbs that end in -ger or -gir, the g in the yo form changes to a j

Pattern 3: -ger / -gir (g → j in the yo form)

For verbs ending in -ger or -gir, the g changes to a j in the yo form to preserve the soft j sound (like the 'h' in "hello").

This spelling change happens because g before a, o, or u has a hard sound (like the 'g' in "go"), but we need the soft sound (like the 'h' in "hello") in the yo form.

Prefixed irregular verbs — rules

Rules for Prefixed Irregular Verbs

  • Many prefixed verbs preserve the irregularities of their base verb.
  • The prefix usually does not change the verb's basic conjugation pattern.
  • Knowing the base verb often helps you predict the prefixed verb's irregular forms.
  • However, these are patterns rather than absolute rules; individual verbs may differ.
