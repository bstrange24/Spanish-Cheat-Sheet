Common Verbs

Spanish verbs change ending for who does the action (conjugation).

Quick start
Idea Spanish term Example Pronunciation English
Dictionary form infinitive hablar = to speak ah-BLAHR to speak
Who does it subject yo, tú, él… yoh, too, ehl
Ending that shows person conjugation hablo = I speak AH-bloh I speak

Three verb families (by infinitive ending)

Type Ending Example Pronunciation English
-ar (most common) -ar hablar ah-BLAHR to speak
-er -er comer koh-MEHR to eat
-ir -ir vivir bee-BEER to live
Present-tense endings (regular)
Person (Subject Pronoun) -ar -er -ir Pronunciation pattern
yo (I) -o -o -o ends in oh
tú (you, informal singular) -as -es -es -as = ahs; -es = ehs
él / ella / usted (he / she / you, formal singular) -a -e -e -a = ah; -e = eh
nosotros / nosotras (we) -amos -emos -imos AH-mohs / EH-mohs / EE-mohs
vosotros / vosotras (you all, Spain) -áis -éis -ís AHEES / EHEES / EES
ellos / ellas / ustedes (they / you all formal) -an -en -en ahn / ehn

1. Subject Pronouns

Used as the subject of a verb (who or what performs the action). They are often omitted in Spanish because the verb ending already shows the person.

English Spanish Notes
I yo
you (informal singular) tú Spain & Latin America
he él
she ella
you (formal singular) usted Uses 3rd-person verb forms
we nosotros / nosotras Gender agreement
you all (informal, Spain) vosotros / vosotras Almost only used in Spain
they ellos / ellas Gender agreement
you all (formal / Latin America) ustedes Used everywhere for plural “you”

2. Object Pronouns

Replace the noun that receives the action.
Direct object pronouns answer “what?” or “whom?”.
Indirect object pronouns answer “to whom?” or “for whom?”.

English Direct Object Pronunciation Indirect Object Pronunciation
me me meh me meh
you (informal singular) te teh te teh
him / it (masculine) lo loh le leh
her / it (feminine) la lah le leh
you (formal singular) lo / la loh / lah le leh
us nos nohs nos nohs
you all (Spain) os ohs os ohs
them los / las lohs / lahs les lehs
you all (formal / Latin America) los / las lohs / lahs les lehs

3. Reflexive Pronouns

Used when the subject does the action to/for itself (or reciprocally: “each other”).

4. Possessive Adjectives & Pronouns

Possessive adjectives come before the noun and agree in number (and sometimes gender).
Possessive pronouns replace the noun entirely and always agree in gender and number with the thing possessed.

Subject pronouns are often dropped — Hablo español AH-bloh ehs-pah-NYOHL 🔊 already means "I speak Spanish."

Regular verbs: full present + pronunciation

• hablar — to speak / to talk → ah-BLAHR

Person Spanish Pronunciation English
yo hablo AH-bloh I speak
tú hablas AH-blahs you speak (informal)
