Common Verbs

Spanish verbs change ending for who does the action (conjugation).
Every form includes pronunciation. Default = LatAm; Spain only if different.

Quick start
Idea Spanish term Example Pronunciation English
Dictionary form infinitive hablar ah-BLAHR = to speak ah-BLAHR to speak
Who does it subject yo yoh, tú too, él ehl… yoh, too, ehl
Ending that shows person conjugation hablo AH-bloh = I speak AH-bloh I speak

1. Subject Pronouns

Used as the subject of a verb (who or what performs the action). They are often omitted in Spanish because the verb ending already shows the person.

English Spanish Notes
I yo yoh
you (informal singular) tú too Spain & Latin America
he él ehl
she ella EH-yah
you (formal singular) usted oos-TEHD Uses 3rd-person verb forms
we nosotros noh-SOH-trohs / nosotras noh-SOH-trahs Gender agreement
you all (informal, Spain) vosotros boh-SOH-trohs / vosotras boh-SOH-trahs Almost only used in Spain
they ellos EH-yohs / ellas EH-yahs Gender agreement
you all (formal / Latin America) ustedes oos-TEH-dehs Used everywhere for plural “you”

2. Object Pronouns

Replace the noun that receives the action.
Direct object pronouns answer “what?” or “whom?”.
Indirect object pronouns answer “to whom?” or “for whom?”.

English Direct Object Indirect Object
me me meh me meh
you (informal singular) te teh te teh
him / it (masculine) lo loh le leh
her / it (feminine) la lah le leh
you (formal singular) lo loh / la lah le leh
us nos nohs nos nohs
you all (Spain) os ohs os ohs
them los lohs / las lahs les lehs
you all (formal / Latin America) los lohs / las lahs les lehs

3. Reflexive Pronouns

Used when the subject does the action to/for itself (or reciprocally: “each other”).

English Reflexive Pronoun Example

4. Possessive Adjectives & Pronouns

Possessive adjectives come before the noun and agree in number (and sometimes gender).
Possessive pronouns replace the noun entirely and always agree in gender and number with the thing possessed.
