Haber / Hay

There is / there are, and the helper verb for perfect tenses

hay — there is / there are

Impersonal hay means there is or there are. It never changes for singular/plural.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Hay un libro. eye oon LEE-broh There is a book.
Hay tres libros. eye trehs LEE-brohs There are three books.
Hay café. eye kah-FEH There is coffee.
¿Hay un banco cerca? eye oon BAHN-koh SEHR-kah Is there a bank nearby?
No hay problema. noh eye proh-BLEH-mah There's no problem.

⚠️ Never say hayan for "there is/are" in the present — only hay.


Other tenses of hay

Still impersonal — one form only.

Tense Form Pronunciation Example Pronunciation English
Present hay eye Hay mucha gente. eye MOO-chah HEHN-teh There are a lot of people.
Imperfect había ah-BEE-ah Había un parque aquí. ah-BEE-ah oon PAHR-keh ah-KEE There was a park here. / There used to be…
Preterite hubo OO-boh Hubo un accidente. OO-boh oon ahk-see-DEHN-teh There was an accident. (completed event)
Future habrá ah-BRAH Habrá una fiesta. ah-BRAH OO-nah FYEHS-tah There will be a party.
Conditional habría ah-BREE-ah Habría más tiempo. ah-BREE-ah mahs TYEHM-poh There would be more time.
Present perfect ha habido ah ah-BEE-doh Ha habido un cambio. ah ah-BEE-doh oon KAHM-byoh There has been a change.

Había = background / used to be there. Hubo = a specific event took place.


hay vs está / están

Hay = something exists / is available.
Está / Están = you already have a specific thing in mind, and you say where it is.

Ask yourself… Use Example Pronunciation English
Does it exist here? Is there one? hay Hay un libro en la mesa. eye oon LEE-broh ehn lah MEH-sah There is a book on the table.
Where is this known thing? está El libro está en la mesa. ehl LEE-broh ehs-TAH ehn lah MEH-sah The book is on the table.
Are there people at the party? hay Hay mucha gente en la fiesta. eye MOO-chah HEHN-teh ehn lah FYEHS-tah There are a lot of people at the party.
Where are my friends? están Mis amigos están en la fiesta. mees ah-MEE-gohs ehs-TAHN ehn lah FYEHS-tah My friends are at the party.
Is there coffee? hay ¿Hay café? eye kah-FEH Is there coffee?
Where is the coffee? está El café está en la cocina. ehl kah-FEH ehs-TAH ehn lah koh-SEE-nah The coffee is in the kitchen.
haber as auxiliary (he / has / ha…)

Perfect tenses: haber + past participle (-ado / -ido).
The participle stays the same; only haber conjugates.

Person Present of haber Pronunciation Example Pronunciation English
yo he eh He comido. eh koh-MEE-doh I have eaten.
tú has ahs Has comido. ahs koh-MEE-doh You have eaten.
vos (LatAm) has ahs Has comido. ahs koh-MEE-doh You have eaten.
él / ella / usted ha ah Ha comido. ah koh-MEE-doh He/she has eaten.
nosotros / nosotras hemos EH-mohs Hemos comido. EH-mohs koh-MEE-doh We have eaten.
vosotros / vosotras (Spain) habéis ah-BEH-ees Habéis comido. ah-BEH-ees koh-MEE-doh You all have eaten. (Spain)
ellos / ellas / ustedes han ahn Han comido. ahn koh-MEE-doh They have eaten.

Regular participles:
hablar → hablado ah-BLAH-doh
comer → comido koh-MEE-doh
vivir → vivido bee-BEE-doh


Common irregular participles:
hecho EH-choh (done/made)  |  dicho DEE-choh (said)  |  visto BEES-toh (seen)  |  escrito ehs-KREE-toh (written)  |  puesto PWEHS-toh (put)  |  vuelto BWEHL-toh (returned)  |  roto ROH-toh (broken)


⚠️ Pronoun placement: Nothing goes between haber and the participle.
He visto la película. eh BEES-toh lah peh-LEE-koo-lah
La he visto. lah eh BEES-toh (pronoun goes before the helper)
He la visto. (incorrect — don't split them)


haber vs tener
Verb Job Example Pronunciation English
haber existence (hay) or helper Hay un problema. / He comido. eye oon proh-BLEH-mah / eh koh-MEE-doh There is a problem. / I have eaten.
tener possession, age, obligation Tengo un problema. TEHN-goh oon proh-BLEH-mah I have a problem. (it's mine)

Incorrect: He un libro. (sounds like "I have eaten a book" or just wrong)
Correct: Tengo un libro. TEHN-goh oon LEE-broh


Haber tips
  • hay = there is / there are. One form. Never changes.
  • Past existence: había (background / used to be) vs hubo (a specific event happened).
  • Future: habrá. Conditional: habría.
  • Location of a known thing → está / están, not hay.
  • Perfect tenses: he eh / has ahs / ha ah / hemos EH-mohs / habéis ah-BEH-ees (Spain) / han ahn + participle.
  • Don't split the helper and the participle. Pronouns go in front: Lo he hecho. loh eh EH-choh (not he lo hecho).
  • "I have" as possession is tengo TEHN-goh, not he eh.
