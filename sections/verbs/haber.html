Haber / Hay

There is / there are, and the helper verb for perfect tenses

hay — there is / there are

Impersonal hay means there is or there are. It never changes for singular/plural.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Hay un libro. 🔊 eye oon LEE-broh There is a book.
Hay tres libros. 🔊 eye trehs LEE-brohs There are three books.
Hay café. 🔊 eye kah-FEH There is coffee.
¿Hay un banco cerca? 🔊 eye oon BAHN-koh SEHR-kah Is there a bank nearby?
No hay problema. 🔊 noh eye proh-BLEH-mah There's no problem.

Never say hayan tres libros for “there are.” Existence in the present is just hay.

Other tenses of hay

Still impersonal — one form only.

Tense Form Pronunciation Example English
Present hay eye Hay mucha gente. 🔊 There are a lot of people.
Imperfect había ah-BEE-ah Había un parque aquí. 🔊 There was a park here. / There used to be…
Preterite hubo OO-boh Hubo un accidente. 🔊 There was an accident. (completed event)
Future habrá ah-BRAH Habrá una fiesta. 🔊 There will be a party.
Conditional habría ah-BREE-ah Habría más tiempo. 🔊 There would be more time.
Present perfect ha habido ah ah-BEE-doh Ha habido un cambio. 🔊 There has been a change.

Había = background / used to be there. Hubo = a specific event took place.

hay vs está / están

Hay = something exists / is available.
Está / están = you already have a specific thing in mind, and you say where it is.

Ask yourself… Use Example English
Does it exist here? Is there one? hay Hay un libro en la mesa. 🔊 There is a book on the table.
Where is this known thing? está El libro está en la mesa. 🔊 The book is on the table.
Are there people at the party? hay Hay mucha gente en la fiesta. 🔊 There are a lot of people at the party.
Where are my friends? están Mis amigos están en la fiesta. 🔊 My friends are at the party.
Is there coffee? hay ¿Hay café? 🔊 Is there coffee?
Where is the coffee? está El café está en la cocina. 🔊 The coffee is in the kitchen.
haber as auxiliary (he / has / ha…)

Perfect tenses: haber + past participle (-ado / -ido).
The participle stays the same; only haber conjugates.

Person Present of haber Example
yo (I) he
I have		 He comido. 🔊
I have eaten.
tú (you, informal) has
you have		 Has comido. 🔊
You have eaten.
vos (you, informal, LatAm) has
you have		 Has comido. 🔊
You have eaten.
él (he) / ella (she) / usted (you, formal) ha
he/she has / you have		 Ha comido. 🔊
He/she has eaten.
nosotros (we, masc./mixed) / nosotras (we, fem.) hemos
we have		 Hemos comido. 🔊
We have eaten.
vosotros (you all, masc./mixed, Spain) / vosotras (you all, fem., Spain) habéis
you all have		 Habéis comido. 🔊
You all have eaten. (Spain)
ellos (they, masc./mixed) / ellas (they, fem.) / ustedes (you all, formal / LatAm) han
they have / you all have		 Han comido. 🔊
They have eaten.

Pronunciation: eh, ahs, ah, EH-mohs, ah-BEH-ees, ahn.

Participles: hablar → hablado; comer → comido; vivir → vivido.
Common irregulars: hecho (done), dicho (said), visto (seen), escrito (written), puesto (put), vuelto (returned), roto (broken).

Nothing goes between haber and the participle: He visto la película. 🔊 (not he la visto — pronouns go before: La he visto. 🔊)

haber vs tener
Verb Job Example English
haber existence (hay) or helper Hay un problema. 🔊 / He comido. 🔊 There is a problem. / I have eaten.
tener possession, age, obligation Tengo un problema. 🔊 I have a problem. (it's mine)

English “I have a book” = Tengo un libro, not he un libro.

Haber tips
  • hay = there is / there are. One form. Pronunciation: eye.
  • Past existence: había (background) vs hubo (an event happened). Future: habrá.
  • Location of a known thing → está / están, not hay.
  • Perfect tenses: he / has / ha / hemos / habéis (Spain) / han + participle.
  • Don't split the helper and the participle. Pronouns go in front: Lo he hecho. 🔊
  • “I have” as possession is tengo, not he.
