Haber ah-BEHR / Hay eye

There is / there are, and the helper verb for perfect tenses

hay — there is / there are

Impersonal hay eye means there is or there are. It never changes for singular/plural.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Hay un libro. eye oon LEE-broh 🔊 eye oon LEE-broh There is a book.
Hay tres libros. eye trehs LEE-brohs 🔊 eye trehs LEE-brohs There are three books.
Hay café. eye kah-FEH 🔊 eye kah-FEH There is coffee.
¿Hay un banco cerca? eye oon BAHN-koh SEHR-kah 🔊 eye oon BAHN-koh SEHR-kah Is there a bank nearby?
No hay problema. noh eye proh-BLEH-mah 🔊 noh eye proh-BLEH-mah There's no problem.

Never say hayan tres libros for “there are.” Existence in the present is just hay eye.

Other tenses of hay

Still impersonal — one form only.

Tense Form Pronunciation Example English
Present hay eye eye Hay mucha gente. eye MOO-chah HEHN-teh 🔊 There are a lot of people.
Imperfect había ah-BEE-ah ah-BEE-ah Había un parque aquí. ah-BEE-ah oon PAHR-keh ah-KEE 🔊 There was a park here. / There used to be…
Preterite hubo OO-boh OO-boh Hubo un accidente. OO-boh oon ahs-see-DEHN-teh 🔊 There was an accident. (completed event)
Future habrá ah-BRAH ah-BRAH Habrá una fiesta. ah-BRAH OO-nah FYEHS-tah 🔊 There will be a party.
Conditional habría ah-BREE-ah ah-BREE-ah Habría más tiempo. ah-BREE-ah mahs TYEHM-poh 🔊 There would be more time.
Present perfect ha habido ah ah-BEE-doh ah ah-BEE-doh Ha habido un cambio. ah ah-BEE-doh oon KAHM-byoh 🔊 There has been a change.

Había ah-BEE-ah = background / used to be there. Hubo OO-boh = a specific event took place.

hay vs está / están

Hay eye = something exists / is available.
Está ehs-TAH / están ehs-TAHN = you already have a specific thing in mind, and you say where it is.

Ask yourself… Use Example English
Does it exist here? Is there one? hay eye Hay un libro en la mesa. eye oon LEE-broh ehn lah MEH-sah 🔊 There is a book on the table.
Where is this known thing? está ehs-TAH El libro está en la mesa. ehl LEE-broh ehs-TAH ehn lah MEH-sah 🔊 The book is on the table.
Are there people at the party? hay eye Hay mucha gente en la fiesta. eye MOO-chah HEHN-teh ehn lah FYEHS-tah 🔊 There are a lot of people at the party.
Where are my friends? están ehs-TAHN Mis amigos están en la fiesta. mees ah-MEE-gohs ehs-TAHN ehn lah FYEHS-tah 🔊 My friends are at the party.
Is there coffee? hay eye ¿Hay café? eye kah-FEH 🔊 Is there coffee?
Where is the coffee? está ehs-TAH El café está en la cocina. ehl kah-FEH ehs-TAH ehn lah koh-SEE-nah 🔊 The coffee is in the kitchen.
haber as auxiliary (he / has / ha…)

Perfect tenses: haber ah-BEHR + past participle (-ado / -ido).
The participle stays the same; only haber ah-BEHR conjugates.

Person Present of haber ah-BEHR Example

Pronunciation: eh, ahs, ah, EH-mohs, ah-BEH-ees, ahn.

Participles: hablar ah-BLAHR → hablado ah-BLAH-doh; comer koh-MEHR → comido koh-MEE-doh; vivir bee-BEER → vivido bee-BEE-doh.
Common irregulars: hecho EH-choh (done), dicho DEE-choh (said), visto BEES-toh (seen), escrito ehs-KREE-toh (written), puesto PWEHS-toh (put), vuelto BWEHL-toh (returned), roto ROH-toh (broken).

Nothing goes between haber ah-BEHR and the participle: He visto la película. eh BEES-toh lah peh-LEE-koo-lah 🔊 (not he la visto — pronouns go before: La he visto. lah eh BEES-toh 🔊)

haber vs tener
Verb Job Example English
haber ah-BEHR existence (hay eye) or helper Hay un problema. eye oon proh-BLEH-mah 🔊 / He comido. eh koh-MEE-doh 🔊 There is a problem. / I have eaten.
tener teh-NEHR possession, age, obligation Tengo un problema. TEHN-goh oon proh-BLEH-mah 🔊 I have a problem. (it's mine)

English “I have a book” = Tengo un libro TEHN-goh oon LEE-broh, not he un libro.

Haber tips
  • hay eye = there is / there are. One form. Pronunciation: eye.
  • Past existence: había ah-BEE-ah (background) vs hubo OO-boh (an event happened). Future: habrá ah-BRAH.
  • Location of a known thing → está ehs-TAH / están ehs-TAHN, not hay eye.
  • Perfect tenses: he eh / has ahs / ha ah / hemos EH-mohs / habéis ah-BEH-ees (Spain) / han ahn + participle.
  • Don't split the helper and the participle. Pronouns go in front: Lo he hecho. loh eh EH-choh 🔊
  • “I have” as possession is tengo TEHN-goh, not he eh.
