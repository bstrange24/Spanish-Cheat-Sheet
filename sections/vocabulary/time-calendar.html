Prepositions of time

Essential prepositions used with time expressions.

Spanish Pronunciation English
a ah at (specific time)
de deh of / from
desde DEHS-deh since / from
durante doo-RAHN-teh during
en ehn in / on (months, seasons, years)
hasta AHS-tah until
por pohr for (duration)
Example sentences:
  • Nos vemos a las tres. nohs BEH-mohs ah lahs trehs
    We'll meet at three o'clock.
  • Trabajo de lunes a viernes. trah-BAH-hoh deh LOO-nehs ah BYEHR-nehs
    I work from Monday to Friday.
  • Vivo aquí desde 2018. BEE-boh ah-KEE DEHS-deh dohs meel dyez-oh-cho
    I've lived here since 2018.
  • Estudia durante la mañana. ehs-TOO-dyah doo-RAHN-teh lah mah-NYAH-nah
    He/She studies during the morning.
  • Nevó en enero. neh-BOH ehn eh-NEH-roh
    It snowed in January.
  • Trabajo hasta las cinco. trah-BAH-hoh AHS-tah lahs SEEN-koh
    I work until five o'clock.
  • Voy a Madrid por tres días. boy ah mah-DREED pohr trehs DEE-ahs
    I'm going to Madrid for three days.

Tips:
A + time = at (specific clock time): a las ocho ah lahs OH-choh (at 8).
En + month/season/year = in/on: en verano ehn beh-RAH-noh (in summer).
Desde = starting point in time; hasta = ending point.
Por + duration = for: por dos horas pohr dohs OH-rahs (for two hours).

Time & calendar tips
  • Use Es la una ehs lah OO-nah for 1:00; all other hours take Son las sohn lahs + number.
  • Add minutes with y ee after the hour; use menos MEH-nohs to count back from the next hour: las tres menos cuarto lahs trehs MEH-nohs KWAHR-toh = 2:45.
  • Clock times often add de la mañana deh lah mah-NYAH-nah / de la tarde deh lah TAHR-deh / de la noche deh lah NOH-cheh instead of a.m./p.m.
  • Days are not capitalized: lunes LOO-nehs, not Lunes. Same for months.
  • To say “on Monday,” use the article: el lunes ehl LOO-nehs. “On Mondays” (habit) is los lunes lohs LOO-nehs.
  • Dates: el cinco de mayo ehl SEEN-koh deh MAH-yoh (day + de + month). Years are read as a full number: dos mil veintiséis dohs meel bayn-tee-SAYS (2026).
  • mañana mah-NYAH-nah means both “morning” and “tomorrow” — context (or por la mañana por lah mah-NYAH-nah) makes it clear.
