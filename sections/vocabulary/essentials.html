Essential Spanish Sayings & Filler Words

Everyday expressions, connectors, and conversational glue

Common Fillers & Connectors

These words don't always translate directly, but they are essential for natural, fluent conversation.

Saying Pronunciation Meaning / Use Example English
pues pwehs Well / so / then — filler, used to pause or transition Pues, no sé. 🔊 Well, I don't know.
bueno BWEH-noh Well / okay / so — filler to start a sentence or show agreement Bueno, vamos. 🔊 Okay, let's go.
o sea oh SEH-ah I mean / in other words / that is — used to clarify or rephrase O sea, no me gusta. 🔊 I mean, I don't like it.
entonces ehn-TOHN-sehs So / then / therefore — logical connector Entonces, ¿qué hacemos? 🔊 So, what do we do?
es decir ehs deh-SEER That is / I mean — used to specify or explain Es decir, no podemos. 🔊 That is, we can't.
en realidad ehn reh-ah-lee-DAHD Actually / in reality — to correct or add nuance En realidad, no es así. 🔊 Actually, it's not like that.
la verdad lah behr-DAHD To be honest / the truth is — softens a statement La verdad, no sé. 🔊 To be honest, I don't know.
a ver ah behr Let's see / let me see — used to think or to ask for attention A ver, explícame. 🔊 Let's see, explain to me.
claro KLAH-roh Of course / clearly — agreement or emphasis Claro que sí. 🔊 Of course yes.
vale BAH-leh Okay / got it (very common in Spain) Vale, de acuerdo. 🔊 Okay, agreed.
listo LEES-toh Ready / okay (used in Latin America for "okay") Listo, ya entendí. 🔊 Okay, I got it now.
dale DAH-leh Go ahead / okay / let's do it (common in Argentina, Uruguay, Caribbean) Dale, vamos. 🔊 Okay, let's go.
ajá ah-HAH Uh-huh / yeah — acknowledgment while listening Ajá, sigo. 🔊 Uh-huh, I'm following.
Opinion & Attitude Expressions

Phrases to express personal views, uncertainty, or to soften what you say.

Saying Pronunciation Meaning / Use Example English
creo que KREH-oh keh I think that / I believe that Creo que sí. 🔊 I think so.
no creo que noh KREH-oh keh I don't think that (often triggers subjunctive) No creo que venga. 🔊 I don't think he's coming.
me parece meh pah-REH-seh It seems to me / I think Me parece bien. 🔊 That seems good to me.
pienso que PYEHN-soh keh I think that (more deliberate than creo que) Pienso que es importante. 🔊 I think it's important.
tal vez tahl behs Maybe / perhaps (often with subjunctive) Tal vez venga mañana. 🔊 Maybe he'll come tomorrow.
quizás kee-SAHS Perhaps / maybe (synonym of tal vez) Quizás tengas razón. 🔊 Perhaps you're right.
a lo mejor ah loh meh-HOR Maybe / perhaps (more colloquial, does NOT trigger subjunctive) A lo mejor viene. 🔊 Maybe he'll come.
seguro que seh-GOO-roh keh Surely / for sure Seguro que sí. 🔊 For sure yes.
es que ehs keh It's just that / the thing is — to justify or explain Es que no tengo tiempo. 🔊 It's just that I don't have time.
lo que pasa es que loh keh PAH-sah ehs keh The thing is that / what happens is that Lo que pasa es que llegué tarde. 🔊 The thing is I arrived late.
en mi opinión ehn mee oh-pee-NYOHN In my opinion En mi opinión, es mejor. 🔊 In my opinion, it's better.
Agreeing, Disagreeing & Reacting

Quick ways to agree, disagree, or show surprise.

Saying Pronunciation Meaning / Use Example English
de acuerdo deh ah-KWEHR-doh Agreed / okay Estoy de acuerdo. 🔊 I agree.
por supuesto por soo-PWEHS-toh Of course Por supuesto que sí. 🔊 Of course yes.
ni hablar nee ah-BLAHR No way / out of the question ¿Ir yo? ¡Ni hablar! 🔊 Me go? No way!
qué va keh bah No way / not at all (used to disagree gently) ¿Estás enojado? — ¡Qué va! 🔊 Are you angry? — Not at all!
claro que sí KLAH-roh keh SEE Of course yes Claro que sí, te ayudo. 🔊 Of course yes, I'll help you.
claro que no KLAH-roh keh NOH Of course not Claro que no me olvidé. 🔊 Of course I didn't forget.
no importa noh eem-POR-tah It doesn't matter No importa, lo hacemos después. 🔊 It doesn't matter, we'll do it later.
no pasa nada noh PAH-sah NAH-dah No problem / it's nothing No pasa nada, tranquilo. 🔊 No problem, don't worry.
qué bien keh BYEHN How nice! / Great! ¿Aprobaste? ¡Qué bien! 🔊 You passed? Great!
qué mal keh MAHL How awful! / That's too bad Perdimos. — ¡Qué mal! 🔊 We lost. — That's too bad.
qué lástima keh LAHS-tee-mah What a pity / shame No pudo venir. — Qué lástima. 🔊 He couldn't come. — What a pity.
qué suerte keh SWEHR-teh How lucky! Gané el sorteo. — ¡Qué suerte! 🔊 I won the raffle. — How lucky!
en serio ehn SEH-ryoh Really? / Seriously — surprise or disbelief ¿En serio? No sabía. 🔊 Really? I didn't know.
no me digas noh meh DEE-gahs You don't say! / No way! (surprise) Se casó. — ¡No me digas! 🔊 He got married. — No way!
Time & Sequence Expressions

Words to organize your ideas and tell stories.

Saying Pronunciation Meaning / Use Example English
primero pree-MEH-roh First / firstly Primero, vamos al banco. 🔊 First, let's go to the bank.
luego LWEH-goh Then / later Luego, fuimos al cine. 🔊 Then, we went to the movies.
después dehs-PWEHS After / afterwards Después de comer, salimos. 🔊 After eating, we went out.
mientras MYEHN-trahs While / meanwhile Mientras tú trabajas, yo descanso. 🔊 While you work, I rest.
entonces ehn-TOHN-sehs So / then (also used for sequence) Entonces, ¿qué pasó? 🔊 So, what happened?
al final ahl fee-NAHL In the end / finally Al final, decidimos quedarnos. 🔊 In the end, we decided to stay.
por fin por FEEN Finally / at last (with relief) ¡Por fin llegaste! 🔊 You finally arrived!
a veces ah BEH-sehs Sometimes A veces voy al gimnasio. 🔊 Sometimes I go to the gym.
de vez en cuando deh behs ehn KWAHN-doh Once in a while De vez en cuando salimos a cenar. 🔊 Once in a while we go out for dinner.
más tarde mahs TAHR-deh Later / later on Te llamo más tarde. 🔊 I'll call you later.
Quick Comparison — Similar Sayings

Understanding the subtle differences between commonly confused expressions.

Saying Meaning When to use Example
creo que I think / I believe Everyday opinion, less certain Creo que sí. 🔊
pienso que I think More deliberate, thought-out opinion Pienso que deberíamos esperar. 🔊
me parece que It seems to me Based on perception, less certain Me parece que va a llover. 🔊
tal vez Maybe Formal, often triggers subjunctive Tal vez venga. 🔊
quizás Maybe Similar to tal vez, also triggers subjunctive Quizás tenga razón. 🔊
a lo mejor Maybe More informal, does NOT trigger subjunctive A lo mejor viene. 🔊
por supuesto Of course Emphatic agreement Por supuesto que sí. 🔊
claro que sí Of course yes Affirmative, obvious agreement Claro que sí, te ayudo. 🔊
no importa It doesn't matter Dismissive, it's not important No importa, olvídalo. 🔊
no pasa nada No problem Reassuring, "it's all good" No pasa nada, tranquilo. 🔊
