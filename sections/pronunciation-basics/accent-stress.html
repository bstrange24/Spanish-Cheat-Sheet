Accent & Stress

Where the loud syllable is, and when Spanish writes an accent mark (tilde)

Default stress (no written accent needed)

If a word has no written accent, stress still follows two default rules:

Ending Stress falls on Example Pronunciation English
vowel, n, or s second-to-last syllable casa KAH-sah, hablan AH-blahn, lunes LOO-nehs KAH-sah, AH-blahn, LOO-nehs house, they speak, Monday
any other consonant last syllable español ehs-pah-NYOHL, ciudad syoo-DAHD, reloj reh-LOHH ehs-pah-NYOHL, syoo-DAHD, reh-LOH Spanish, city, clock/watch
Written accent = the rule is broken

A written accent (á ah é eh í ee ó oh ú oo) marks the stressed syllable when it is not where the default rule would put it.

Word Why the accent Pronunciation English
café kah-FEH ends in vowel but stress is last kah-FEH coffee
teléfono teh-LEH-foh-noh stress is third-from-last teh-LEH-foh-noh telephone
canción kahn-SYOHN ends in n but stress is last kahn-SYOHN (LatAm) / kahn-THYOHN (Spain) song
fácil FAH-seel ends in l so default would be last; stress is earlier FAH-seel (LatAm) / FAH-theel (Spain) easy
árbol AHR-bohl ends in l; stress is first syllable AHR-bohl tree
Accents that change meaning (same letters)
Without accent With accent
si see = if sí see = yes
el ehl = the él ehl = he
tu too = your tú too = you
te teh = you (object) té teh = tea
mi mee = my mí mee = me (after a preposition)
se seh = himself / herself / themselves sé seh = I know
de deh = of / from dé deh = give (subjunctive / usted command)
solo SOH-loh = alone (can also mean only) sólo SOH-loh = only (older spelling; now often written solo SOH-loh)
Question words always take an accent

When they ask a question (or an indirect question), these words have a written accent:

Question word Pronunciation English Not a question (no accent)
qué keh kay what que keh = that / which
cómo KOH-moh KOH-moh how como KOH-moh = like / as / I eat
cuándo KWAHN-doh KWAHN-doh when cuando KWAHN-doh = when (as a connector)
dónde DOHN-deh DOHN-deh where donde DOHN-deh = where (as a connector)
quién KYEHN kyehn who quien KYEHN = who (as a connector)

Example: ¿Cómo estás? KOH-moh ehs-TAHS 🔊 vs Como pan. KOH-moh pahn 🔊

Accents that split a diphthong (hiatus)

An accent on í or ú next to another vowel usually means two syllables, not a diphthong. See also Diphthongs.

Word Pronunciation English
día DEE-ah DEE-ah day
tío TEE-oh TEE-oh uncle
país pah-EES pah-EES country
baúl bah-OOL bah-OOL trunk / chest
Accent & stress tips
  • The accent mark does not change the vowel sound. a ah and á ah are the same AH — the mark only shows which syllable is loud.
  • Plural can move or keep the accent: canción kahn-SYOHN → canciones kahn-SYOH-nehs (accent dropped because stress now follows the default).
  • If you can hear the stress, you can usually decide whether the written accent belongs.
