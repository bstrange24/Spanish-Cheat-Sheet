Pronouns (1st, 2nd, 3rd person)

Spanish pronouns change by person (who), number (one / more), and sometimes gender (masc. / fem.).
Formal "you" is 3rd-person forms (usted oos-TEHD / ustedes oos-TEH-dehs), even though it means "you."

Pronunciation of these pronouns is the same in LatAm and Spain.
The big regional difference is usage: Spain uses vosotros boh-SOH-trohs / os ohs / vuestro BWEHS-troh for informal "you all"; Latin America almost always uses ustedes oos-TEH-dehs instead.

Person map (who is who)
Person English idea Singular Plural
1st I / we I we
2nd you you (informal) you all (informal, mainly Spain)
3rd he / she / it / formal you / they / them he, she, formal you they, them, formal you all
1. Subject pronouns (who does the action)

Subject pronouns are pronouns that identify who or what is performing the action of a verb.
Spanish often drops them because the verb ending already shows the person (hablo AH-bloh = I speak).

3rd singular él ehl he ehl Accent; without accent el ehl = "the"

3. Indirect object pronouns (to/for whom)

These mark to whom / for whom something is done (I give the book to her).

Person Spanish English Pronunciation
1st singular me meh to/for me meh
2nd singular te teh to/for you teh
3rd singular le leh to/for him, her, you formal leh
1st plural nos nohs to/for us nohs
2nd plural (Spain) os ohs to/for you all ohs
3rd plural les lehs to/for them, you all lehs

le leh/les lehs → se seh when stacked with a direct object lo loh/la lah/los lohs/las lahs:
