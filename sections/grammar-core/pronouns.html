Pronouns (1st, 2nd, 3rd person)

Spanish pronouns change by person (who), number (one / more), and sometimes gender (masc. / fem.).

Pronunciation of these pronouns is the same in LatAm and Spain.
The big regional difference is usage:
Spain uses vosotros / os / vuestro for informal "you all";
Latin America almost always uses ustedes instead.

Person map (who is who)
Person English Singular Plural
1st I / we I we
2nd you you (informal) you all (informal, mainly Spain)
3rd he / she / it / formal you / they / them he, she, formal you they, them, formal you all
1. Subject pronouns (who does the action)

Subject pronouns are pronouns that identify who or what is performing the action of a verb.
Spanish often drops them because the verb ending already shows the person (hablo = I speak).

Singular Plural
yo (I) nosotros (we, masc./mixed)
nosotras (we, fem.)

él (he)
ella (she)
usted (you, formal) 		ellos (they, masc./mixed)
ellas (they, fem.)
ustedes (you all, formal / LatAm)

3. Indirect object pronouns (to/for whom)

These mark to whom / for whom something is done (I give the book to her).

5. Prepositional pronouns (after a preposition: a, de, para, con…)

Used after words like a, de, para, sin, por. Most look like subject pronouns, but mí / ti are special, and con has special forms.

él (him)
ella (her)
usted (you - formal)
ello(it - abstract)
ellos (them, masc./mixed)
ellas (them, fem.)
