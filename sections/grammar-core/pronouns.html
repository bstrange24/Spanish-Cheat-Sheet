Pronouns (1st, 2nd, 3rd person)

Spanish pronouns change by person (who), number (one / more), and sometimes gender (masc. / fem.).
Formal "you" is 3rd-person forms (usted / ustedes), even though it means "you."

Pronunciation of these pronouns is the same in LatAm and Spain.
The big regional difference is usage: Spain uses vosotros / os / vuestro for informal "you all"; Latin America almost always uses ustedes instead.

Person map (who is who)
Person English idea Singular Plural
1st I / we I we
2nd you you (informal) you all (informal, mainly Spain)
3rd he / she / it / formal you / they / them he, she, formal you they, them, formal you all
1. Subject pronouns (who does the action)

Subject pronouns are pronouns that identify who or what is performing the action of a verb.
Spanish often drops them because the verb ending already shows the person (hablo = I speak).

Person Spanish English Pronunciation Notes
1st singular yo I yoh
2nd singular (informal) tú you too Friends, family, kids; needs accent
2nd singular (informal, some regions) vos you bohs Used in parts of Latin America (Argentina, etc.)
3rd singular él he ehl Accent; without accent el = "the"
3rd singular ella she EH-yah
3rd singular (formal you) usted (Ud.) you (formal) oos-TEHD Uses 3rd-person verb forms
1st plural nosotros we (masc. / mixed) noh-SOH-trohs
1st plural nosotras we (all female) noh-SOH-trahs
2nd plural (informal, Spain) vosotros you all (masc. / mixed) boh-SOH-trohs Mainly Spain
2nd plural (informal, Spain) vosotras you all/ you are/ you're (all female) boh-SOH-trahs Mainly Spain
3rd plural ellos they/ they are/ They're (masc. / mixed) EH-yohs
3rd plural ellas they/ they are/ They're (all female) EH-yahs
3rd plural (formal / LatAm "you all") ustedes (Uds.) you all oos-TEH-dehs LatAm: all plural "you." Spain: formal plural "you" (informal = vosotros)

3. Indirect object pronouns (to/for whom)

These mark to whom / for whom something is done (I give the book to her).

Person Spanish English Pronunciation
1st singular me to/for me meh
2nd singular te to/for you teh
3rd singular le to/for him, her, you formal leh
1st plural nos to/for us nohs
2nd plural (Spain) os to/for you all ohs
3rd plural les to/for them, you all lehs

le/les → se when stacked with a direct object lo/la/los/las:

  • Se lo doy 🔊 → seh loh doy → I give it to him/her/you/them

4. Reflexive pronouns (the action reflects back on the subject)

Used with verbs like llamarse (to call oneself), levantarse (to get up).

1st singular me myself meh me llamo → meh YAH-moh (LatAm / many Spain) / meh LYAH-moh (trad. Spain)
2nd singular te yourself teh te lavas → teh LAH-bahs (you wash yourself)
3rd singular se himself / herself / yourself (formal) seh se levanta → seh leh-BAHN-tah
1st plural nos ourselves nohs nos vemos 🔊 → nohs BEH-mohs (see you / we see ourselves)
2nd plural (Spain) os yourselves ohs os ducháis 🔊 → ohs doo-CHAHEES (Spain only usage)
3rd plural se themselves / yourselves seh se llaman 🔊 → seh YAH-mahn (LatAm / many Spain) / seh LYAH-mahn (trad. Spain)

5. Prepositional pronouns (after a preposition: a, de, para, con…)

Used after words like a, de, para, sin, por. Most look like subject pronouns, but mí / ti are special, and con has special forms.

Person Spanish English Pronunciation Notes
1st singular mí me mee Accent; para mí = for me
2nd singular ti you tee No accent; para ti = for you
3rd singular él him ehl
3rd singular ella her EH-yah
3rd singular usted you (formal) oos-TEHD
3rd singular (neutral) ello it (abstract) EH-yoh Less common in speech
1st plural nosotros / nosotras us noh-SOH-trohs / -trahs
2nd plural (Spain) vosotros / vosotras you all boh-SOH-trohs / -trahs
3rd plural ellos / ellas them EH-yohs / EH-yahs
3rd plural ustedes you all oos-TEH-dehs
