Nouns

All Spanish nouns have gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural).

Masculine nouns typically end in -o, -e, or consonants.
Feminine nouns typically end in -a, -ción, -sión, -dad, -tad, -tud, or -umbre.
Neuter article lo is used for abstract ideas, concepts, and unspecified things.

In this sheet: Masculine nouns are marked in blue, feminine in pink, and the neuter article in green.
Not color-coded: nouns that are the same for both genders (el artista / la artista) or words that don't follow the typical patterns.

Example sentences:
  • el hombre ehl OHM-breh
    the man
  • la mujer lah moo-HEHR
    the woman
  • el agua ehl AH-gwah
    the water (feminine noun, masculine article)
  • el águila ehl AH-gee-lah
    the eagle (feminine noun, masculine article)
  • los libros lohs LEE-brohs
    the books
  • las casas lahs KAH-sahs
    the houses

Tip: Feminine nouns that begin with a stressed a- sound take el in the singular to avoid the awkward "la a" sound. They are still feminine nouns — use feminine adjectives with them: el agua fría ehl AH-gwah FREE-ah (the cold water).

Neuter article & "lo" (abstract concepts)
Article Use Example Pronunciation English
lo Abstract nouns (with adjectives) lo importante loh eem-por-TAHN-teh the important thing
lo With possessive pronouns lo mío loh MEE-oh mine / what's mine
lo With relative clauses lo que digo loh kay DEE-goh what I say / the thing that I say
lo With adverbs lo de ayer loh deh ah-YEHR what happened yesterday / the thing about yesterday
lo Emphasizing adjectives lo más + adjective loh mahs the most (thing)
Example sentences:
  • Lo importante es estudiar. loh eem-por-TAHN-teh ehs ehs-too-DYAR
    The important thing is to study.
  • Lo mío es tuyo. loh MEE-oh ehs TOO-yoh
    What's mine is yours.
  • Lo que digo es verdad. loh kay DEE-goh ehs behr-DAHD
    What I say is true.
  • Lo de ayer fue raro. loh deh ah-YEHR fweh RAH-roh
    What happened yesterday was strange.
  • Lo más importante. loh mahs eem-por-TAHN-teh
    The most important thing.

Key rules for "lo":

  • Lo is neuter — it has no plural form and is never used with nouns
  • It turns adjectives, possessives, or entire phrases into abstract nouns
  • It refers to ideas, concepts, or unspecified things — never to specific people or objects

⚠️ Important exceptions:

  • Nouns ending in -d or -z are often feminine (e.g., la ciudad, la voz)
  • Nouns ending in -a are usually feminine, except for Greek-origin words ending in -ma, -pa, -ta (e.g., el problema, el mapa, el planeta)
  • Accented vowel endings (á, é, í, ó, ú) are generally masculine, even if they end in -a
