Nouns (sustantivos)

Gender, articles, and plural formation

Nouns - gender & articles

All Spanish nouns have gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural).
Masculine nouns typically end in -o, -e, or consonants.
Feminine nouns typically end in -a, -ción, -sión, -dad, -tad, -tud, or -umbre.
Neuter article lo is used for abstract ideas, concepts, and unspecified things.

In this sheet: Masculine nouns are marked in blue, feminine in pink, and the neuter article in green.

Examples: el hombre ehl OHM-breh (the man), la mujer lah moo-HEHR (the woman), lo importante loh eem-por-TAHN-teh (the important thing), el agua AH-gwah (feminine noun with masculine article), los libros lohs LEE-brohs (the books), las casas lahs KAH-sahs (the houses) 🔊

Not color-coded: nouns that are the same for both genders (el artista ehl ahr-TEES-tah / la artista lah ahr-TEES-tah) or words that don't follow the typical patterns.

Neuter article & "lo" (abstract concepts)
Article Use Example Pronunciation English
lo loh Abstract nouns (with adjectives) lo importante loh eem-por-TAHN-teh loh eem-por-TAHN-teh the important thing
lo loh With possessive pronouns lo mío loh MEE-oh loh MEE-oh mine / what's mine
lo loh With relative clauses lo que digo loh keh DEE-goh loh kay DEE-goh what I say / the thing that I say
lo loh With adverbs lo de ayer loh deh ah-YEHR loh deh ah-YEHR what happened yesterday / the thing about yesterday
lo loh Emphasizing adjectives lo más loh mahs + adjective loh mahs the most (thing)

Key rules for "lo":

  • Lo is neuter — it has no plural form and is never used with nouns
  • It turns adjectives, possessives, or entire phrases into abstract nouns
  • It refers to ideas, concepts, or unspecified things — never to specific people or objects

⚠️ Important exceptions:

  • Nouns ending in -d or -z are often feminine (e.g., la ciudad lah syoo-DAHD, la voz lah bohs)
  • Nouns ending in -a are usually feminine, except for Greek-origin words ending in -ma, -pa, -ta (e.g., el problema, el mapa, el planeta)
  • Accented vowel endings (á, é, í, ó, ú) are generally masculine, even if they end in -a
