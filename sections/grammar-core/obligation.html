Obligation

Must, have to, should — tener que, hay que, deber

Three common patterns
Pattern Who is obliged Example Pronunciation English
tener que + infinitive a specific person (the subject) Tengo que trabajar. 🔊 TEHN-goh kay trah-bah-HAHR I have to work.
hay que + infinitive people in general (“one must”) Hay que estudiar. 🔊 eye kay ehs-too-DYAHR You/one has to study. (general rule)
deber + infinitive a specific person; duty or “should” Debes descansar. 🔊 DEH-behs dehs-kahn-SAHR You should / must rest.
Tener que — present
Person Form Example English
yo tengo que Tengo que irme. 🔊 I have to leave.
tú tienes que Tienes que llamar. 🔊 You have to call.
vos tenés que Tenés que comer. 🔊 You have to eat. (LatAm voseo)
él / ella / usted tiene que Ella tiene que estudiar. 🔊 She has to study.
nosotros tenemos que Tenemos que pagar. 🔊 We have to pay.
vosotros tenéis que Tenéis que esperar. 🔊 You all have to wait. (Spain)
ellos / ustedes tienen que Tienen que llegar temprano. 🔊 They / you all have to arrive early.
Deber — present
Person Form Pronunciation English
yo debo DEH-boh I should / I must / I owe
tú debes DEH-behs you should
él / usted debe DEH-beh he/she/you should
nosotros debemos deh-BEH-mohs we should
vosotros debéis deh-BEH-ees you all should (Spain)
ellos / ustedes deben DEH-behn they / you all should

Debería (conditional) is a softer “should”: Deberías dormir más. 🔊

Obligation tips
  • Hay que never changes: not *hayo que, not *hemos que.
  • Tener que is the everyday “I gotta.” Deber can sound more like advice or moral duty.
  • Need: necesitar is “to need,” not quite as strong as have-to: Necesito agua.
  • See also Tener for more tener que examples.
