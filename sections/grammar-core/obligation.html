Obligation

Must, have to, should — tener que teh-NEHR keh, hay que eye keh, deber deh-BEHR

Three common patterns
Pattern Who is obliged
tener que + infinitive a specific person (the subject)
hay que + infinitive people in general ("one must")
deber + infinitive a specific person; duty or "should"
Example sentences:
  • Tengo que trabajar. TEHN-goh kay trah-bah-HAHR
    I have to work.
  • Hay que estudiar. eye kay ehs-too-DYAHR
    You/one has to study. (general rule)
  • Debes descansar. DEH-behs dehs-kahn-SAHR
    You should / must rest.

Tener que — present
Person Form
yo tengo que
tú tienes que
vos tenés que
él / ella / usted tiene que
nosotros tenemos que
vosotros tenéis que
ellos / ustedes tienen que
Example sentences:
  • Tengo que irme. TEHN-goh kee EER-meh
    I have to leave.
  • Tienes que llamar. TYEH-nehs keh yah-MAHR
    You have to call.
  • Tenés que comer. teh-NEHS keh koh-MEHR
    You have to eat. (LatAm voseo)
  • Ella tiene que estudiar. EH-yah TYEH-neh keh ehs-too-DYAHR
    She has to study.
  • Tenemos que pagar. teh-NEH-mohs keh pah-GAHR
    We have to pay.
  • Tenéis que esperar. teh-NEH-ees keh ehs-peh-RAHR
    You all have to wait. (Spain)
  • Tienen que llegar temprano. TYEH-nehn keh yeh-GAHR tehm-PRAH-noh
    They / you all have to arrive early.

Hay que — present

Hay que + infinitive = one must / you have to (general obligation, no specific person).

Form Use
hay que + infinitive people in general ("one must")
Example sentences:
  • Hay que estudiar para el examen. eye kay ehs-too-DYAHR PAH-rah ehl ehk-SAH-mehn
    One must study for the exam.
  • Hay que comer frutas y verduras. eye kay koh-MEHR FROO-tahs ee behr-DOO-rahs
    You have to eat fruits and vegetables.
  • Hay que llegar a tiempo. eye kay yeh-GAHR ah TYEHM-poh
    One must arrive on time.
  • Hay que beber agua todos los días. eye kay beh-BEHR AH-gwah TOH-dohs lohs DEE-ahs
    You have to drink water every day.
  • Hay que respetar las reglas. eye kay rehs-peh-TAHR lahs REH-glahs
    One must respect the rules.

Tip: Hay que is impersonal — it doesn't change for person or number. It's used for general advice, rules, or things that "have to be done" without specifying who should do them.
Compare with tener que (specific person):
✅ Hay que estudiar. eye kay ehs-too-DYAHR (general — everyone should study)
✅ Tengo que estudiar. TEHN-goh keh ehs-too-DYAHR (specific — I have to study)


Deber — present
Person Form
yo debo
tú debes
él / ella / usted debe
nosotros debemos
vosotros debéis
ellos / ustedes deben
Example sentences:
  • Debo estudiar más. DEH-boh ehs-too-DYAHR mahs
    I should study more.
  • Debes descansar. DEH-behs dehs-kahn-SAHR
    You should rest.
  • Ella debe comer mejor. EH-yah DEH-beh koh-MEHR meh-HOR
    She should eat better.
  • Debemos llegar a tiempo. deh-BEH-mohs yeh-GAHR ah TYEHM-poh
    We should arrive on time.
  • Debéis beber agua. deh-BEH-ees beh-BEHR AH-gwah
    You all should drink water. (Spain)
  • Deben escuchar. DEH-behn ehs-koo-CHAHR
    They / you all should listen.

Tip: Debería deh-beh-REE-ah (conditional) is a softer "should":
Deberías dormir más. deh-beh-REE-ahs dohr-MEER mahs
You should sleep more. (softer suggestion)


Obligation tips
  • Hay que never changes: not *hayo que, not *hemos que.
  • Tener que is the everyday "I gotta." Deber can sound more like advice or moral duty.
  • Need: necesitar is "to need," not quite as strong as have-to: Necesito agua.
  • See also Tener for more tener que examples.
