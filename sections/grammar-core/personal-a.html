Personal A

The extra a before people and pets when they are direct objects

When to use personal a

If the direct object is a specific person (or a pet you treat like a person), put a before it. It does not translate as "to."

Spanish Pronunciation English
Veo a Juan. 🔊 BEH-oh ah hwahn I see Juan.
Visito a mi abuela. 🔊 bee-SEE-toh ah mee ah-BWEH-lah I visit my grandmother.
Busco a María. 🔊 BOOS-koh ah mah-REE-ah I'm looking for María.
Conozco a tu hermano. 🔊 koh-NOHS-koh ah too ehr-MAH-noh (LatAm) / koh-NOHTH-koh (Spain) I know your brother.
¿A quién llamas? 🔊 ah kyehn YAH-mahs Who are you calling?
Quiero a mi perro. 🔊 KYEH-roh ah mee PEH-rroh I love my dog.

Groups of people still take it: Ayudo a los estudiantes. ah-YOO-doh ah lohs ehs-too-DYAHN-tehs 🔊

Not used with tener

Possession with tener does not take personal a, even with people.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Tengo un hermano. 🔊 TEHN-goh oon ehr-MAH-noh I have a brother. (no a)
Tengo dos hijos. 🔊 TEHN-goh dohs EE-hohs I have two children.
¿Tienes un médico? 🔊 TYEH-nehs oon MEH-dee-koh Do you have a doctor?

Contrast: Busco a un médico. BOOS-koh ah oon MEH-dee-koh 🔊 (looking for a specific doctor) vs Tengo un médico. TEHN-goh oon MEH-dee-koh 🔊 (I have a doctor).

a + el = al

a + masculine singular el contracts to al. No contraction with la, los, or las.

Form Example Pronunciation English
al = a + el Veo al profesor. 🔊 BEH-oh ahl proh-feh-SOHR I see the (male) teacher.
a la (no contraction) Veo a la profesora. 🔊 BEH-oh ah lah proh-feh-SOH-rah I see the (female) teacher.
a los Veo a los profesores. 🔊 BEH-oh ah lohs proh-feh-SOH-rehs I see the teachers.
a las Veo a las profesoras. 🔊 BEH-oh ah lahs proh-feh-SOH-rahs I see the (female) teachers.
Also used with…
Case Example English
Someone / nobody ¿Ves a alguien? 🔊 behs ah AHL-hyehn / No veo a nadie. 🔊 noh BEH-oh ah NAH-dyeh Do you see anyone? / I don't see anyone.
Who? as direct object ¿A quién ves? 🔊 ah KYEHN behs Who do you see?
Personified things (sometimes) Quiero mucho a mi país. 🔊 KYEH-roh MOO-choh ah mee pah-EES I love my country a lot.
Indefinite / unspecified person (often skipped) Necesito un médico. 🔊 neh-seh-SEE-toh oon MEH-dee-koh I need a doctor (any doctor).

If the person is specific, keep a: Necesito al médico de Ana. neh-seh-SEE-toh ahl MEH-dee-koh deh AH-nah 🔊

Personal A tips
  • Specific person or beloved pet as direct object → add a: Veo a Ana. BEH-oh ah AH-nah 🔊
  • Thing as direct object → no a: Veo el coche. BEH-oh ehl KOH-cheh 🔊
  • Tener (possession) never takes personal a: Tengo dos hermanos. TEHN-goh dohs ehr-MAH-nohs 🔊
  • a + el = al: al profesor ahl proh-feh-SOHR.
  • nadie NAH-dyeh / alguien AHL-hyehn / quién KYEHN as objects: a nadie ah NAH-dyeh, a alguien ah AHL-hyehn, ¿a quién? ah KYEHN
  • This a is not the "to" of Voy a casa boy ah KAH-sah. Same spelling, different job.
