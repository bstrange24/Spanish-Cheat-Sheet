Personal A

The extra a before people and pets when they are direct objects

When to use personal a

If the direct object is a specific person (or a pet you treat like a person), put a ah before it. It does not translate as “to.”

Spanish Pronunciation English
Veo a Juan. BEH-oh ah hwahn 🔊 BEH-oh ah hwahn I see Juan.
Visito a mi abuela. bee-SEE-toh ah mee ah-BWEH-lah 🔊 bee-SEE-toh ah mee ah-BWEH-lah I visit my grandmother.
Busco a María. BOOS-koh ah mah-REE-ah 🔊 BOOS-koh ah mah-REE-ah I'm looking for María.
Conozco a tu hermano. koh-NOHS-koh ah too ehr-MAH-noh 🔊 koh-NOHS-koh ah too ehr-MAH-noh (LatAm) / koh-NOHTH-koh (Spain) I know your brother.
¿A quién llamas? ah kyehn YAH-mahs 🔊 ah kyehn YAH-mahs Who are you calling?
Quiero a mi perro. KYEH-roh ah mee PEH-rroh 🔊 KYEH-roh ah mee PEH-rroh I love my dog.
People vs things
Person / pet (use a ah) Thing (no a ah)
Veo a la profesora. BEH-oh ah lah proh-feh-SOH-rah 🔊
I see the teacher.		 Veo el libro. BEH-oh ehl LEE-broh 🔊
I see the book.
Busco a mi amigo. BOOS-koh ah mee ah-MEE-goh 🔊
I'm looking for my friend.		 Busco un libro. BOOS-koh oon LEE-broh 🔊
I'm looking for a book.
Oigo a los niños. OY-goh ah lohs NEE-nyohs 🔊
I hear the children.		 Oigo la música. OY-goh lah MOO-see-kah 🔊
I hear the music.
Llamo a Ana. YAH-moh ah AH-nah 🔊
I call Ana.		 Llamo un taxi. YAH-moh oon TAHK-see 🔊
I call a taxi.

Groups of people still take it: Ayudo a los estudiantes. ah-YOO-doh ah lohs ehs-too-DYAHN-tehs 🔊

Not used with tener

Possession with tener teh-NEHR does not take personal a ah, even with people.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Tengo un hermano. TEHN-goh oon ehr-MAH-noh 🔊 TEHN-goh oon ehr-MAH-noh I have a brother. (no a ah)
Tengo dos hijos. TEHN-goh dohs EE-hohs 🔊 TEHN-goh dohs EE-hohs I have two children.
¿Tienes un médico? TYEH-nehs oon MEH-dee-koh 🔊 TYEH-nehs oon MEH-dee-koh Do you have a doctor?

Contrast: Busco a un médico. BOOS-koh ah oon MEH-dee-koh 🔊 (looking for a specific doctor) vs Tengo un médico. TEHN-goh oon MEH-dee-koh 🔊 (I have a doctor).

a + el = al

a ah + masculine singular el ehl contracts to al ahl. No contraction with la lah, los lohs, or las lahs.

Form Example Pronunciation English
al ahl = a ah + el ehl Veo al profesor. BEH-oh ahl proh-feh-SOHR 🔊 BEH-oh ahl proh-feh-SOHR I see the (male) teacher.
a la ah lah (no contraction) Veo a la profesora. BEH-oh ah lah proh-feh-SOH-rah 🔊 BEH-oh ah lah proh-feh-SOH-rah I see the (female) teacher.
a los ah lohs Veo a los profesores. BEH-oh ah lohs proh-feh-SOH-rehs 🔊 BEH-oh ah lohs proh-feh-SOH-rehs I see the teachers.
a las ah lahs Veo a las profesoras. BEH-oh ah lahs proh-feh-SOH-rahs 🔊 BEH-oh ah lahs proh-feh-SOH-rahs I see the (female) teachers.
Also used with…
Case Example English
Someone / nobody ¿Ves a alguien? behs ah AHL-hyehn 🔊 / No veo a nadie. noh BEH-oh ah NAH-dyeh 🔊 Do you see anyone? / I don't see anyone.
Who? as direct object ¿A quién ves? ah KYEHN behs 🔊 Who do you see?
Personified things (sometimes) Quiero mucho a mi país. KYEH-roh MOO-choh ah mee pah-EES 🔊 I love my country a lot.
Indefinite / unspecified person (often skipped) Necesito un médico. neh-seh-SEE-toh oon MEH-dee-koh 🔊 I need a doctor (any doctor).

If the person is specific, keep a ah: Necesito al médico de Ana. neh-seh-SEE-toh ahl MEH-dee-koh deh AH-nah 🔊

Personal A tips
  • Specific person or beloved pet as direct object → add a ah: Veo a Ana. BEH-oh ah AH-nah 🔊
  • Thing as direct object → no a ah: Veo el coche. BEH-oh ehl KOH-cheh 🔊
  • Tener teh-NEHR (possession) never takes personal a ah: Tengo dos hermanos. TEHN-goh dohs ehr-MAH-nohs 🔊
  • a ah + el ehl = al ahl: al profesor ahl proh-feh-SOHR.
  • nadie NAH-dyeh / alguien AHL-hyehn / quién KYEHN as objects: a nadie ah NAH-dyeh, a alguien ah AHL-hyehn, ¿a quién? ah KYEHN
  • This a ah is not the “to” of Voy a casa boy ah KAH-sah. Same spelling, different job.
