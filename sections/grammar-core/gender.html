Gender

Every noun is masculine or feminine

Gender (el / la)

In Spanish, every noun is masculine or feminine.
Articles (el / la), adjectives, and many pronouns must match that gender (and singular/plural).

This sits here on purpose: possessive forms like el mío / la mía only make sense once you know gender.

Why el agua?
Feminine words that start with a stressed a- / ha- sound take el in the singular (easier to say), but stay feminine:

  • el agua fría → ehl AH-gwah FREE-ah → the cold water (fría is feminine)
  • las aguas → lahs AH-gwahs → the waters (plural back to las)

Gender tips
  • Learn nouns with the article: el libro, la casa — not just libro / casa.
  • -o often masculine, -a often feminine — but watch exceptions (el día, la mano, el problema).
  • Plural mixed groups usually use masculine: los niños, los amigos.
  • Adjectives match the noun: casa blanca, libro rojo.
  • Speaker gender shows up in soy alto/alta, estoy cansado/cansada, encantado/encantada.
  • Possessives like mío/mía match the owned thing, not the owner.
  • el agua, el águila: singular el, but adjective stays feminine (agua fría).

Pronoun tips
  • tú (you) has an accent; tu (your) does not.
  • él (he) has an accent; el (the) does not.
  • mí (me, after preposition) has an accent; mi (my) does not.
  • Subject pronouns are optional: Hablo español already means "I speak Spanish."
  • LatAm: everyday plural "you" = ustedes (not vosotros).
  • Spain: informal plural "you" = vosotros; formal plural = ustedes.
  • su / suyo is ambiguous (his/her/your/their) — Spanish often clarifies with de él, de ella, de usted , etc.
