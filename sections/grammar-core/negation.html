Negation

Saying no, never, nothing, nobody — and double negatives

no before the verb

Put no immediately before the conjugated verb.

Spanish Pronunciation English
No hablo francés. noh AH-bloh frahn-SEHS (LatAm) / frahn-THEHS (Spain) I don't speak French.
No estoy cansado. noh ehs-TOY kahn-SAH-doh I'm not tired.
No he comido. noh eh koh-MEE-doh I haven't eaten.
No quiero ir. noh KYEH-roh eer I don't want to go.
No, gracias. noh GRAH-syahs (LatAm) / GRAH-thyahs (Spain) No, thank you.

Object pronouns stay attached to the verb group: No lo veo. noh loh BEH-oh — I don't see it.
No me gusta. noh meh GOOS-tah — I don't like it.

Example sentences:
  • Nunca fumo. NOON-kah FOO-moh
    I never smoke.
  • No veo nada. noh BEH-oh NAH-dah
    I don't see anything.
  • No vino nadie. noh BEE-noh NAH-dyeh
    Nobody came.
  • No tengo ningún libro. noh TEHN-goh neen-GOON LEE-broh
    I don't have any book.
  • No hay ninguna silla. noh eye neen-GOO-nah SEE-yah
    There isn't any chair.
  • Yo tampoco voy. yoh tahm-POH-koh boy
    I'm not going either.
  • Ya no vivo allí. yah noh BEE-boh ah-YEE
    I no longer live there.
  • Todavía no llego. toh-dah-BEE-ah noh YEH-goh
    I haven't arrived yet.
  • No lo haré jamás. noh loh ah-REH hah-MAHS
    I will never do it.
  • No come ni carne ni pescado. noh KOH-meh nee KAHR-neh nee pehs-KAH-doh
    He/She doesn't eat meat or fish.

Double negation

Spanish requires a second negative after the verb. No veo nada is correct — not "I don't see nothing" as an error.

Spanish Pronunciation English
No veo nada. noh BEH-oh NAH-dah I don't see anything.
No conozco a nadie. noh koh-NOHS-koh ah NAH-dyeh (LatAm) / koh-NOHTH-koh (Spain) I don't know anybody.
No voy nunca. noh boy NOON-kah I never go.
No tengo ningún problema. noh TEHN-goh neen-GOON proh-BLEH-mah I don't have any problem.
No me gusta tampoco. noh meh GOOS-tah tahm-POH-koh I don't like it either.

You can stack them: No le digo nunca nada a nadie. noh leh DEE-goh NOON-kah NAH-dah ah NAH-dyeh — I never tell anybody anything.


Placement

If the negative word is before the verb, drop extra no
If it is after the verb, keep no

Negative first (no extra no) Verb first (need no) English
Nunca voy. NOON-kah boy No voy nunca. noh boy NOON-kah I never go.
Nadie vino. NAH-dyeh BEE-noh No vino nadie. noh BEE-noh NAH-dyeh Nobody came.
Nada pasó. NAH-dah pah-SOH No pasó nada. noh pah-SOH NAH-dah Nothing happened.
Ninguno funciona. neen-GOO-noh foon-SYOH-nah No funciona ninguno. noh foon-SYOH-nah neen-GOO-noh None of them work.
Tampoco quiero café. tahm-POH-koh KYEH-roh kah-FEH No quiero café tampoco. noh KYEH-roh kah-FEH tahm-POH-koh I don't want coffee either.

Ya no and todavía no keep no next to the verb: Ya no trabajo. yah noh trah-BAH-hoh / Todavía no termino. toh-dah-BEE-ah noh tehr-MEE-noh


ningún / ninguno / ninguna
Form When Example English
ningún before a masculine singular noun ningún libro neen-GOON LEE-broh no book / not any book
ninguno pronoun (stands alone, m.) No quiero ninguno. noh KYEH-roh neen-GOO-noh I don't want any (of them).
ninguna feminine singular (noun or pronoun) ninguna casa neen-GOO-nah KAH-sah no house
ningunos / ningunas rare; usually stay singular No tengo ninguno. noh TEHN-goh neen-GOO-noh I don't have any.

Positive "some / someone / something": alguien AHL-hyehn, algo AHL-goh, algún ahl-GOON / alguno ahl-GOO-noh. Don't mix them with no noh in the same way: not no veo algo → No veo nada. noh BEH-oh NAH-dah


Negation tips
  • Default: no right before the verb: No sé. noh seh
  • After the verb, Spanish loves a second negative: No veo nada. noh BEH-oh NAH-dah
  • Negative word first → no extra no: Nunca voy. NOON-kah boy
  • Nadie NAH-dyeh as a direct object still needs personal a: No veo a nadie. noh BEH-oh ah NAH-dyeh
  • ningún neen-GOON before masc. noun; ninguno neen-GOO-noh alone.
  • ya no yah noh = no longer; todavía no toh-dah-BEE-ah noh = not yet; tampoco tahm-POH-koh = neither.
  • English "I don't see anything" = Spanish "I don't see nothing." That's the rule, not a mistake.
