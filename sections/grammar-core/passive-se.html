Passive / Se

How Spanish says "it is done" or "people do X" with se

Se + 3rd person (the most common "passive")

Use se + a 3rd-person verb. The verb agrees with the thing, not with a person doing it.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Se habla español. 🔊 seh AH-blah ehs-pah-NYOHL Spanish is spoken. / They speak Spanish.
Se vende una casa. 🔊 seh BEHN-deh OO-nah KAH-sah A house is for sale. / House for sale.
Se venden casas. 🔊 seh BEHN-dehn KAH-sahs Houses are for sale.
Se come bien aquí. 🔊 seh KOH-meh byehn ah-KEE You eat well here. / The food is good here.
¿Cómo se dice…? 🔊 KOH-moh seh DEE-seh How do you say…?
Impersonal se (people in general)

When no specific thing is the subject, se + singular verb often means "one / people / you (in general)."

Spanish Pronunciation English
Se vive bien en este pueblo. 🔊 seh BEE-beh byehn ehn EHS-teh PWEH-bloh People live well in this town.
No se puede fumar. 🔊 noh seh PWEH-deh foo-MAHR You can't smoke. / No smoking.
Accidental se (it happened to me)

Se me olvidó seh meh ohl-bee-DOH = "It slipped my mind" (not "I forgot on purpose"). The thing forgotten is the subject.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Se me olvidó la llave. 🔊 seh meh ohl-bee-DOH lah YAH-beh I forgot the key. (it slipped my mind)
Se me olvidaron las llaves. 🔊 seh meh ohl-bee-DAH-rohn lahs YAH-behs I forgot the keys.
Se le cayó el vaso. 🔊 seh leh kah-YOH ehl BAH-soh He/she dropped the glass. (it fell on them)
Se nos acabó el pan. 🔊 seh nohs ah-kah-BOH ehl pahn We ran out of bread.
Ser + participle (more formal passive)

Like English "was written." Less common in everyday speech than se.

Spanish Pronunciation English
El libro fue escrito en 1990. 🔊 ehl LEE-broh fweh ehs-KREE-toh ehn The book was written in 1990.
La casa fue construida por mi abuelo. 🔊 lah KAH-sah fweh kohns-tree-EE-dah por mee ah-BWEH-loh The house was built by my grandfather.
Passive / se tips
  • Signs and labels love this: Se alquila seh ahl-KEE-lah (for rent), Se busca seh BOOS-kah (wanted / hiring).
  • Plural thing → plural verb: se venden seh BEHN-dehn, not *se vende casas.
  • This se is not the same as a reflexive "wash yourself," even though the word looks identical. Context decides.
