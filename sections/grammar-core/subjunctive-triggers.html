Subjunctive Triggers

When to use the subjunctive — you already have the tense pages; this is the when

The basic shape

Two clauses, different subjects, and a trigger in the first clause:
Quiero que vengas. KYEH-roh keh BEHN-gahs — I want you to come.

Indicative (fact) Pronunciation Subjunctive (wish / doubt / emotion) Pronunciation
Sé que vienes. seh keh BYEH-nehs Dudo que vengas. DOO-doh keh BEHN-gahs
Es verdad que trabaja. ehs behr-DAHD keh trah-BAH-hah Es posible que trabaje. ehs poh-SEE-bleh keh trah-BAH-heh

Same subject → infinitive, not subjunctive: Quiero ir. KYEH-roh eer (I want to go.) vs Quiero que vayas. KYEH-roh keh BAH-yahs (I want you to go.)

Conjunctions that often take subjunctive
Conjunction Pronunciation Example Pronunciation English
para que PAH-rah kay Te lo explico para que entiendas. teh loh ehks-PLEE-koh PAH-rah keh ehn-TYEHN-dahs I'm explaining it so that you understand.
antes de que AHN-tehs deh kay Sal antes de que llueva. sahl AHN-tehs deh keh YWEH-bah Leave before it rains.
sin que seen kay Salió sin que yo lo viera. sah-LYOH seen keh yoh loh BYEH-rah He left without my seeing him.
cuando (future / not yet) KWAHN-doh Llámame cuando llegues. YAH-mah-meh KWAHN-doh YEH-gehs Call me when you arrive. (not yet)
aunque (unknown / contrary) OWN-kay Aunque llueva, voy. OWN-keh YWEH-bah boy Even if it rains, I'm going.
Subjunctive trigger tips
  • Full forms: Present Subjunctive and Imperfect Subjunctive in the Tenses menu.
  • Creo que KREH-oh keh + indicative. No creo que noh KREH-oh keh + subjunctive.
  • After a past trigger, Spanish often uses imperfect subjunctive: Quería que vinieras. keh-REE-ah keh bee-NYEH-rahs
