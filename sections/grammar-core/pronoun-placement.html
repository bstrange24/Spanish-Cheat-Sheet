Pronoun Placement

Where me, te, lo, se go — before the verb, or stuck on the end

Default: before a conjugated verb
Spanish Pronunciation English
Lo veo. loh BEH-oh I see it / him.
Te llamo. teh YAH-moh I'll call you.
No lo sé. noh loh seh I don't know (it).
Se lo doy. seh loh doy I give it to him/her/you.

le + lo cannot sit together → se lo (not *le lo).


Attach to infinitive, gerund, or affirmative command
Type Example Pronunciation English
Infinitive Voy a verlo. / Lo voy a ver. boy ah BEHR-loh / loh boy ah behr I'm going to see it.
Gerund (-ndo) Estoy viéndolo. / Lo estoy viendo. ehs-TOY BYEHN-doh-loh / loh ehs-TOY BYEHN-doh I'm seeing it.
Affirmative command Dímelo. (must attach) DEE-meh-loh Tell it to me.
Negative command No me lo digas. (must stay in front) noh meh loh DEE-gahs Don't tell me that.
Order when two pronouns appear

Indirect before direct: se + te/me/nos + lo/la/los/las is not the usual pair — the usual pair is indirect + direct: me lo meh loh, te la teh lah, se los seh lohs.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Me lo das. meh loh dahs You give it to me.
Tienes que dármelo. TYEH-nehs kay DAHR-meh-loh You have to give it to me.
Dádmelo. (vosotros) DAHD-meh-loh Give it to me. (Spain, you all)

Written accent is often added when pronouns lengthen the word: da dah → dámelo DAH-meh-loh.


Pronoun placement tips
  • See Pronouns for the full lists (direct, indirect, reflexive).
  • Negative words stay in front: nunca lo hago NOON-kah loh AH-goh.
  • Don't double-place: not *lo voy a verlo.
