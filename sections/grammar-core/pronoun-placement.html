Pronoun Placement

Where me meh, te teh, lo loh, se seh go — before the verb, or stuck on the end

Default: before a conjugated verb
Spanish Pronunciation English
Lo veo. loh BEH-oh 🔊 loh BEH-oh I see it / him.
Te llamo. teh YAH-moh 🔊 teh YAH-moh I’ll call you.
No lo sé. noh loh seh 🔊 noh loh seh I don’t know (it).
Se lo doy. seh loh doy 🔊 seh loh doy I give it to him/her/you.

le leh + lo loh cannot sit together → se lo seh loh (not *le lo).

Attach to infinitive, gerund, or affirmative command
Type Two options (or one) English
Infinitive Voy a verlo. boy ah BEHR-loh / Lo voy a ver. loh boy ah behr 🔊 I’m going to see it.
Gerund (-ndo) Estoy viéndolo. ehs-TOY BYEHN-doh-loh / Lo estoy viendo. loh ehs-TOY BYEHN-doh 🔊 I’m seeing it.
Affirmative command Dímelo. DEE-meh-loh 🔊 (must attach) Tell it to me.
Negative command No me lo digas. noh meh loh DEE-gahs 🔊 (must stay in front) Don’t tell me that.
Order when two pronouns appear

Indirect before direct: se + te/me/nos + lo/la/los/las is not the usual pair — the usual pair is indirect + direct: me lo meh loh, te la teh lah, se los seh lohs.

Spanish Pronunciation English
Me lo das. meh loh dahs 🔊 meh loh dahs You give it to me.
Tienes que dármelo. TYEH-nehs kay DAHR-meh-loh 🔊 TYEH-nehs kay DAHR-meh-loh You have to give it to me.
Dádmelo. DAHD-meh-loh 🔊 (vosotros) DAHD-meh-loh Give it to me. (Spain, you all)

Written accent is often added when pronouns lengthen the word: da dah → dámelo DAH-meh-loh.

Pronoun placement tips
  • See Pronouns for the full lists (direct, indirect, reflexive).
  • Negative words stay in front: nunca lo hago NOON-kah loh AH-goh.
  • Don’t double-place: not *lo voy a verlo.
